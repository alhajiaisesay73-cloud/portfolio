Client build

Casa Locé Winery

Live in production

An AI concierge that answers questions about the wine, recommends the right tasting, and books it. Five workflows on n8n, all monitored.

They had an agent already. It was slow, it could not actually book, and it sent people to pages that did not exist. Tastings run from 45 dollars a head to 200, so a lost booking is not a lost lead. It is somewhere between 90 and 1,600 dollars that was already interested enough to start talking.

It knows who it is talking to

Before the AI is involved at all, the system looks the guest up and routes on what it finds. New, returning, already booked, or disqualified. Someone who has already reserved a table gets a warm reply, not another sales pitch.

The model is fenced in

  • It knows the date. Real Los Angeles time is fetched and injected, so "this Friday" resolves correctly.
  • The reply format is a contract. Structured JSON every turn, with a fallback that wraps plain text instead of crashing.
  • It cannot book on its own say-so. Code re-checks all five details before a booking fires.
  • It only offers real times. Actual availability per tier, per weekday.
Something I found going back through it

The chat agent tags guests in HubSpot as "Web Chat". The dashboard searches for "web_chat". Those do not match, so the reporting has shown nothing this whole time while everything else ran fine.

It is a one-value fix and I have taken it to them with a recommendation to backfill rather than start from zero. I am putting it in my own portfolio because you are going to hire someone who ships bugs either way. What you want to know is whether they go back and look, and whether they tell you.

Client build

The Echo Journal

5 stars, became a retainer

An echocardiography education brand with 73,000 followers on Instagram and 48,000 on Facebook, and a newsletter the audience was not finding.

The platform limitation that shaped everything

ManyChat has no Substack integration, and more importantly it cannot detect whether somebody actually subscribed. So the obvious design does not work. You cannot send a link, wait, and follow up only with the people who ignored it, because that information does not exist on the ManyChat side.

I built the confirmation into the conversation instead. A button that says they have just subscribed, which tags them and pulls them out of follow-up. It depends on somebody pressing a button, so it is not perfect, and I describe it as exactly that. But it fails in the safe direction: a subscriber who skips the button gets one more nudge, not a week of them.

Work out your own number

Take your follower count, and the share who would join your list if somebody asked properly at the moment they were already talking to you. Multiply by what a subscriber is worth to you over a year.

That is what an unbuilt funnel costs you annually, and it is the only number that decides whether this is worth doing.

Own build

Instagram DM Lead Qualification

My own system, not a client deployment

Seven workflows that take somebody from a comment on a post to a booked call, and then prove which comment produced which booking.

It closes the attribution loop

Every fifteen minutes the system pulls recent Calendly bookings, opens each one and reads the tracking field carried through from the original DM. That value is the Instagram sender ID, so the booking matches straight back to the conversation that produced it.

Most coaches can tell you how many DMs they got and how many calls they had. Very few can tell you which DM became which call. Attribution has to be built in at the first touch. It cannot be added later and backfilled.

The last follow-up says it is the last one

Quiet leads get Day 1, 3, 5 and 7 messages, each written for that person from their own answers. The Day 7 message is instructed to acknowledge that life gets busy, make one sincere invitation, and say plainly that this is the final follow-up.

Most sequences just go silent. Telling someone the door is open and you are going to stop messaging is both more honest and more likely to get a reply.

Own build

AI Customer Support Agent

Built and running, no named client

Reads support email, looks up the real answer in the store's own systems, and replies. When it cannot answer properly, it hands over to a human instead of guessing.

  • It sorts before it answers. A classifier splits order tracking, stock, general questions and everything else, so the agent is never working out what kind of problem it has while also solving it.
  • Three real sources of truth. Live Shopify orders, live Shopify products, and a FAQ vector store. Every answer comes from one of those, never from what the model happens to remember about your business.
  • The knowledge base maintains itself. Update the FAQ document in Drive and the index rebuilds.
  • It escalates instead of inventing. No confident answer, or a customer asking for a person, and the email goes to Slack with full context.
The question to ask anyone selling you a support bot

Not "how accurate is it". Ask what it does when it does not know.

An agent that answers everything will eventually tell a customer their order shipped when it did not. That one email costs more trust than a hundred fast replies earn.

The through-line

What every one of these has in common

The interesting engineering is never the happy path. It is what the system does when something goes wrong, and whether anyone finds out.

The principleWhat it looks like in the build
State machines, not chatbots Routing on a stored customer status, so a booked customer is never sold to again
The model is never trusted blindly Structured output contracts, parsing that degrades instead of crashing, server-side checks before anything commits
Model choice is a cost decision Sonnet for live conversation where quality is visible, Haiku for follow-ups where it is not
Monitoring ships with the build A dedicated error workflow naming the exact failed node, with a link to that execution
Failure is isolated One flaky third-party API cannot strand a customer halfway through a booking
Attribution is built in on day one The source travels from first touch to closed deal, because it cannot be reconstructed later
